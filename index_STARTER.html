Tigers







About My Animal



After a century of decline, overall wild tiger numbers are starting to tick upward.
Based on the best available information, tiger populations are stable or increasing
inIndia, Nepal, Bhutan, Russia, and China. About 5,574 tigers remain in the wild,
according to the Global Tiger Forum, but much more work is needed to protect this
species if we are to secure its future in the wild. In some areas, including much
of Southeast Asia, tigers are still in crisis and declining in number.






Fun Facts



  • Tigers are the largest wild cats in the world
  • No 2 tigers have the same stripes
  • A tiger's roar can be heard from two to three miles
  • Tigers love water
  • Tigers have a very powerful night vision




A tiger's life cycle



  1. Birth and Infancy - 0 to 2 months
  2. Juvenile Cubhood - 2 to 18 months
  3. Independence and Young Adult - 18 months to 3 years
  4. Adulthood and old age - 3 years to 15+ years

The mission to save tigers

Saving tigers is not just about protecting one magnificent animal;
it means guarding entire ecosystems. When we protect a tiger,
we secure vast forests, clean rivers, and steady water cycles that
support countless other species. In the past, illegal poaching,
hunting, and rapid tree cutting pushed wild tiger numbers to
dangerous lows. Thanks to strict anti-poaching laws, dedicated
wildlife rangers, and expanding protected reserves from just 9
original areas to nearly 60, tiger populations have mounted a historic
comeback. Keeping the tiger safe ensures that the lungs of
our earth remain green, wild, and full of life.
